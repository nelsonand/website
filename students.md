  <div class="body">
    <h2>Mentoring: from structure to independence</h2>
    <p>The principle that guides my teaching also guides my mentoring: <strong>provide structure first, then deliberately transfer ownership</strong>. New students need clear expectations and concrete examples. As they progress, I hand over responsibility so they learn to generate and defend their own research ideas.</p>
    <p>Each student needs a different path to independence. With one graduate student, I started with the hands-off approach I had thrived under myself, but it soon became clear that she needed more structure. We doubled our meeting frequency and I encouraged more communication with collaborators across the field. Within a year she had first-authored her first paper and given an invited talk at a major APS conference. That experience is why I now regularly ask my students for feedback on my mentoring and adjust accordingly.</p>
    <p>In practice, this means individual development plans revisited each semester, structured onboarding with shared code and documentation, paired projects for new students, and paid undergraduate research positions.</p>
